## Post 1 — Dashboard Sprawl
If everyone owns the dashboard, no one owns the decision.

Most BI teams have a dashboard problem, not a data problem.

Here’s the pattern:
1) Dashboards get built per request.
2) No decision owner is assigned.
3) Usage decays but maintenance cost stays.

Result: noisy metrics, conflicting narratives, slower decisions.

A better operating rule:
- Every dashboard must map to one named decision.
- Every decision must have one owner and one review cadence.
- Kill dashboards with no active decision loop.

The goal isn’t fewer dashboards.
It’s faster, cleaner decisions.

How many dashboards would your team shut down this month?

## Post 2 — KPI Trust Debt
Every broken KPI is a tax on every decision.

When metric trust drops, two things happen fast:
- Teams reopen old debates instead of acting.
- Leadership confidence shifts from data to opinion.

That is trust debt, and it compounds like financial debt.

Three fixes that work:
1) Publish metric definitions with clear owners.
2) Add freshness + lineage visibility to executive views.
3) Run a weekly “top 5 metric exceptions” review.

Dashboards don’t fail because charts are ugly.
They fail because users can’t tell what to trust.

If your highest-impact KPI is wrong for 48 hours, what business decision gets delayed?

## Post 3 — AI in BI
AI won’t fix bad metric definitions. It scales confusion faster.

Everyone wants AI copilots in analytics.
Few teams fix the context layer first.

Before adding AI, validate:
- Shared metric dictionary
- Semantic consistency across teams
- Access rules for sensitive business fields

Without this, AI gives fluent answers to the wrong question.
And wrong answers delivered quickly are worse than slow answers.

The stack order matters:
Data quality → semantic clarity → governance → AI interface.

What context does your AI assistant still miss today?
